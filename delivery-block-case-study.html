Preventing Shipment of Unpaid E-commerce Orders

I designed and implemented a final delivery-control rule that prevents unpaid online orders from leaving the warehouse while preserving legitimate ship-first, pay-later workflows for business customers.

Project overview

A targeted safety net at the final fulfillment step

Bank-transfer orders entered the ERP before funds arrived. This created a path where an unpaid order could reach “Ready” status and be shipped by mistake.

The control was intentionally placed at delivery validation rather than at order import. Warehouse teams could still prepare orders, but the system performed a final payment check before goods physically left the business.

The solution also distinguished imported online orders from manually created B2B orders, ensuring customers with approved invoice terms could continue receiving goods before payment.

Constraint

Preserve B2B invoice-term fulfillment

Problem

Unpaid online orders could be completed and shipped

Payment confirmation happened separately from warehouse fulfillment. Without a technical checkpoint, staff could validate a delivery even when no payment had been registered.

Outcome

Online deliveries now require a qualifying paid invoice

The system refuses the validation attempt, preserves the delivery exactly as it was, and allows the same action to succeed immediately after payment is recorded.

Decision logic

Blocking only the risky scenario

The rule activates only when every required condition is true. This avoids introducing friction into unrelated inventory workflows.

01

Outgoing customer delivery

Receipts, returns and internal stock movements are excluded.

02

Validation to completed status

The check runs when a user attempts to finalize the shipment.

03

Related sales order exists

The delivery must be connected to a valid customer order.

04

Imported online order

Manual B2B orders remain exempt from the restriction.

05

No qualifying payment

The order is blocked unless a posted invoice is paid, in payment or reversed.

Design rationale

Why the validation point matters

Blocking every unpaid order at import would have been too broad and would have disrupted valid commercial arrangements. The final delivery step is the precise moment when financial risk becomes physical loss.

By checking the order source and payment state only at that point, the solution achieves strong control with minimal impact on users, inventory preparation or finance operations.

Maintenance principle: the automation can be deactivated temporarily, but should not be deleted. A paid order that still triggers the warning should be treated first as a payment-registration or draft-invoice issue rather than a rule failure.
