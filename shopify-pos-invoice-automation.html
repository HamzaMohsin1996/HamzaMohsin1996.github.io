Confidential Production Case Study

Restoring POS Invoice Automation

I investigated a silent invoice-delivery failure across an ecommerce POS, a third-party integration connector, and an ERP system. Through stakeholder interviews, workflow mapping, source-code analysis, and production testing, I identified the root cause and built a safe automation bridge.

Project Overview

A successful automation that silently skipped the customers who needed it

Customers who completed in-store purchases through the ecommerce POS were not receiving their invoices by email. The scheduled ERP action responsible for sending the invoices ran every two minutes and reported no errors, making the failure difficult to identify.

I analyzed the complete workflow between the ecommerce platform, the integration connector, ERP contacts, accounting invoices, payment journals, and email automation. The investigation revealed that the email existed in Ecommerce and in the connector queue, but was never copied into the newly created ERP contact.

My role

System analysis, stakeholder interviews, debugging, automation design, testing

Project context

Confidential production environment

Project type

Systems

Ecommerce POS, ERP, Integration Connector

Environment

Live production workflow

Problem

Paid POS invoices remained unsent without showing an error

Customers completed their purchase and provided their details, but ERP contacts created from POS orders had no email address. The sending automation required a contact email, so it quietly ignored the invoices.

Outcome

Invoice delivery was restored without modifying vendor code

I implemented a safe email-backfill automation for identified customers and a separate PDF archival process for anonymous POS invoices. A previously failing invoice was then sent automatically.

The Challenge

Why the issue was difficult to diagnose

The visible symptoms suggested an email or scheduled action problem. However, both systems were working correctly. The real failure occurred earlier in the data synchronization process.

01

No visible errors

The scheduled action completed successfully, so the ERP did not report a failure.

02

Only POS customers were affected

Customers created earlier through web orders already had email addresses and continued to work normally.

03

The data appeared to be missing

The email was absent from the ERP contact, although it still existed in the connector's order queue.

04

Different payment types behaved differently

Cash invoices became paid immediately, while card invoices depended on reconciliation.

Stakeholder Research

I interviewed two people involved in the workflow

Before changing the automation, I needed to understand what happened at the till and what happened later in the ERP. The interviews helped separate operational expectations from technical behavior.

The interview findings were combined with production data, system logs, invoice records, connector queue records, and source-code inspection.

Interview 01

Store Operations

Research goal

Understand how customer information, payment, and invoice expectations were handled at the till.

Key findings

  • Customers expected an invoice after payment.
  • Customer information could be attached during the checkout process.
  • Existing and newly created customers did not always behave in the same way.
  • Adding an email after charging could require manual office follow-up.
Interview 02

Finance / Back Office

Research goal

Understand how invoices, payments, reconciliation, and scheduled email actions behaved in the ERP.

Key findings

  • The scheduled action displayed no errors.
  • Manual invoice sending still worked.
  • Unsent invoices were concentrated in the ecommerce POS workflow.
  • Card invoices could remain unpaid until reconciliation.
Investigation Question

At which point between a completed POS purchase and the ERP invoice-sending action was the customer's email being lost?

Technical Investigation

The source code confirmed where the data flow broke

I inspected the third-party connector method responsible for creating customers from Ecommerce POS orders:

Connector location models/res_partner.py → create_shopify_pos_customer

The connector read the customer email into a variable, but that value was not included when the new ERP contact was created. This explained why the email existed in the queue but not on the contact.

Root-cause summary Python / ERP 
# Email is available from Ecommerce
customer_email = shopify_customer_email

partner_values = {
    "name": customer_name,
    "street": customer_street,
    "zip": customer_zip,
    "city": customer_city,
    "phone": customer_phone,

    # The email value is not written here
    # "email": customer_email
}

Solution Design

A safe bridge instead of a risky third-party code change

Changing third-party connector code could complicate future updates. I therefore created two separate, narrowly scoped automations while preparing a permanent bug report for the connector vendor.

Never overwrite existing emails
Never email the shared POS customer
Keep email and anonymous flows separate
Log every automated change
01
Every 5 minutes

Backfill customer emails

Active bridge

This automation reads the customer email from completed connector queue records and writes it to the associated ERP contact only when the contact's email field is empty.

  • Processes completed connector queue records.
  • Validates that the value resembles an email.
  • Checks order and invoice contacts.
  • Skips the shared anonymous POS customer.
  • Never replaces an existing email.
  • Creates an audit log for every update.
02
Every 30 minutes

Archive anonymous POS invoices

Production

Anonymous sales use a generic POS customer without an email address. Instead of attempting delivery, this automation generates the official PDF after the invoice is fully paid and marks it as processed.

  • Processes only posted customer invoices.
  • Requires the payment state to be Paid.
  • Targets only the generic POS customer.
  • Generates the official invoice PDF.
  • Marks the invoice as archived or processed.
  • Logs PDF-generation failures.
Automation Safety

The two invoice flows cannot process the same invoice

I designed the conditions as disjoint sets. One flow handles identified customers who have an email. The other handles only the generic anonymous POS customer, which has no email.

Existing send cron

Identified customer

  • Contact has an email
  • Invoice is posted
  • Invoice is paid
  • Invoice has not been sent
No overlap
Anonymous archive cron

Generic POS customer

  • Contact has no email
  • Invoice is posted
  • Invoice is fully paid
  • PDF has not been archived
Important implementation decision

Generate anonymous PDFs only when an invoice is fully Paid

The filter was changed from Paid or In Payment to Paid only. Cash invoices can be processed immediately, while card invoices wait until bank reconciliation confirms the payment.

Proof of Resolution

A previously failing invoice was sent automatically

Three duplicate contact records for the same customer were merged into the correct record. After the valid email was available on the contact, invoice a previously affected invoice was picked up and sent automatically.

This confirmed that the invoice-generation and email-sending systems were working. The missing contact email was the blocking condition.

Additional Findings

Card invoices require reconciliation

Card invoices may not reach the Paid state until the related bank or payout reconciliation is complete. A send-cron change was prepared but not applied immediately.

Weekend payouts are delayed

Weekend Ecommerce card revenue is included in later business-day payouts. The delay is part of the financial workflow rather than a connector error.

Invoice language follows the contact

Anonymous PDFs were generated in English because the shared POS customer language was English. The contact language can be changed to German when required.

Customer timing matters at the till

Attaching the customer and email before charging produces the most reliable automated workflow. Details added after payment may need manual handling.

Next Steps

The bridge solves the immediate problem while the permanent connector fix remains with the connector provider

  1. 01

    Connector connector fix

    The connector should write the customer email when creating POS contacts and update existing contacts when the email is missing.

  2. 02

    Card-payment decision

    Observe the reconciliation cycle before allowing bank-journal invoices to enter the automatic sending process.

  3. 03

    Duplicate-contact cleanup

    Use case-insensitive email matching and review suggested merges manually because family members can share an email address.

  4. 04

    Improved till process

    Attach the correct customer with an email before charging and add a complete name whenever possible.

Retrospective

What this project taught me

The visible failure was an unsent email, but the true cause was an incomplete data transfer several steps earlier. Solving it required understanding the complete operational and technical system.

Investigate the complete workflow

A failure at the final step can originate much earlier in the data pipeline.

Combine interviews with technical evidence

Stakeholders explained the real process, while logs and source code revealed the implementation.

Prefer reversible production fixes

A narrow automation bridge reduced risk and can be removed after the connector provider fix.

Validate with real scenarios

Existing, new, anonymous, cash, and card cases required separate validation.
